# Protestant Theology Knowledge Base — Trusted Sources

A curated reference of 30 books and 30 websites for answering tough theological
questions from a Protestant frame. Sources are chosen for (a) fidelity to Scripture
as final authority, (b) scholarly/pastoral reputation, and (c) durable online presence.

**How to use this for hard questions:** Protestantism is not monolithic. On contested
points (predestination, baptism, spiritual gifts, eschatology, women in ministry, the
Lord's Supper) different traditions read the same Bible differently. Each entry below is
tagged with a tradition so the system can present *which camp holds a view and why*,
rather than flattening real disagreement. Tradition tags:

- **REF** — Reformed / Calvinist (incl. Presbyterian, Reformed Baptist)
- **LUT** — Lutheran
- **WES** — Wesleyan / Arminian / Methodist
- **BAP** — Baptist / broadly evangelical
- **ANG** — Anglican / Evangelical Anglican
- **ECU** — Broadly Protestant / ecumenical-evangelical (cross-tradition)

Primary authority for *all* of these is the Christian canon of Scripture (sola scriptura);
creeds, confessions, and authors are treated as subordinate witnesses.

## Part 1 — 30 Books

### Systematic & Reference Theology
1. **Wayne Grudem — *Systematic Theology*** (BAP/REF). Accessible, heavily Scripture-cited,
   the most widely used evangelical systematics; each doctrine argued from biblical texts.
2. **Louis Berkhof — *Systematic Theology*** (REF). The standard 20th-c. Reformed systematics;
   rigorous, concise, confessionally Reformed.
3. **Herman Bavinck — *Reformed Dogmatics* (4 vols)** (REF). Deep, irenic, historically aware;
   the gold standard for serious Reformed dogmatics.
4. **John Calvin — *Institutes of the Christian Religion*** (REF). Foundational Reformation
   systematic theology; saturated with Scripture and exegesis.
5. **Charles Hodge — *Systematic Theology* (3 vols)** (REF). Classic Princeton theology;
   thorough on the doctrine of Scripture.
6. **Michael Horton — *The Christian Faith*** (REF). Modern, covenantal, engages contemporary
   philosophy and other traditions fairly.
7. **Millard Erickson — *Christian Theology*** (BAP). Balanced, surveys options before
   concluding; good for seeing the range of evangelical positions.
8. **John Frame — *Systematic Theology*** (REF). Strong on epistemology, the doctrine of God,
   and ethics; presuppositional flavor.
9. **Thomas C. Oden — *Classic Christianity*** (WES). Wesleyan systematics rooted in the
   consensus of the early church fathers; good cross-tradition ballast.
10. **Thomas Watson — *A Body of Divinity*** (REF/Puritan). Warm, practical exposition of the
    Westminster Shorter Catechism.

### Biblical Theology (whole-Bible storyline)
11. **Geerhardus Vos — *Biblical Theology*** (REF). Father of modern Reformed biblical theology;
    traces revelation across redemptive history.
12. **Graeme Goldsworthy — *According to Plan*** (ANG/ECU). Clear introduction to reading the
    whole Bible as one unfolding story centered on Christ.
13. **G.K. Beale — *A New Testament Biblical Theology*** (REF/ECU). Major work on how the OT is
    fulfilled in the NT ("already/not yet").
14. **Vaughan Roberts — *God's Big Picture*** (ANG). Short, lucid overview of the Bible's
    kingdom storyline; excellent on-ramp.

### Apologetics & Worldview
15. **C.S. Lewis — *Mere Christianity*** (ANG/ECU). The most influential popular defense of
    "mere" Christian belief.
16. **Timothy Keller — *The Reason for God*** (REF/ECU). Engages modern skeptic objections
    pastorally and intelligently.
17. **Cornelius Van Til — *The Defense of the Faith*** (REF). Foundational text of
    presuppositional apologetics.
18. **Greg Bahnsen — *Always Ready*** (REF). Practical presuppositional method; strong on the
    impossibility of the contrary.
19. **John Lennox — *Can Science Explain Everything?*** (ECU). Oxford mathematician on
    science–faith; accessible and rigorous.
20. **Francis Schaeffer — *The God Who Is There*** (REF/ECU). Cultural/worldview apologetics;
    diagnoses modern thought.

### Core Doctrine & Spiritual Classics
21. **J.I. Packer — *Knowing God*** (ANG/REF). Modern classic on the doctrine of God and the
    Christian life; deeply biblical.
22. **John Stott — *The Cross of Christ*** (ANG). The definitive evangelical treatment of the
    atonement.
23. **R.C. Sproul — *The Holiness of God*** (REF). On God's holiness and human sin; clear and
    pastoral.
24. **B.B. Warfield — *The Inspiration and Authority of the Bible*** (REF). The classic
    evangelical defense of biblical inspiration/inerrancy.
25. **Martin Luther — *The Bondage of the Will*** (LUT). Luther's central work on grace, sin,
    and the will; the Reformation's theological core.
26. **John Owen — *The Mortification of Sin*** (REF/Puritan). The Puritan masterwork on
    fighting sin by the Spirit.
27. **Jonathan Edwards — *Religious Affections*** (REF). On the nature of genuine vs. false
    religious experience.
28. **John Bunyan — *The Pilgrim's Progress*** (BAP/Puritan). The most-read Protestant
    allegory of the Christian life.
29. **D. Martyn Lloyd-Jones — *Studies in the Sermon on the Mount*** (REF/ECU). Model
    expository preaching; doctrine applied.
30. **Augustine — *Confessions*** (Pre-Reformation, foundational). Not Protestant per se, but
    the theological taproot Luther and Calvin drew on (grace, sin, the will).

> **Confessional documents worth holding alongside these** (primary-source standards many of
> the books expound): the *Westminster Confession of Faith & Catechisms* (REF), the
> *Heidelberg Catechism* and *Belgic Confession* (REF), the *Augsburg Confession* (LUT), the
> *Thirty-Nine Articles* (ANG), the *London Baptist Confession 1689* (BAP), and the *Articles
> of Religion* (WES). These are excellent for "what does tradition X officially hold."

## Part 2 — 30 Websites

### Major teaching ministries
1. **Desiring God** — desiringgod.org (REF; John Piper). Deep archive of articles, sermons,
   and books; God-centered, Scripture-heavy.
2. **Ligonier Ministries** — ligonier.org (REF; R.C. Sproul). Trusted teaching, articles, and
   *Tabletalk* devotional magazine.
3. **The Gospel Coalition** — thegospelcoalition.org (REF/ECU). Broad evangelical hub;
   articles, the peer-reviewed *Themelios* journal, book reviews.
4. **Grace to You** — gty.org (BAP; John MacArthur). Sermons and Q&A; expository, conservative.
5. **9Marks** — 9marks.org (BAP/REF). Ecclesiology — church health, membership, polity.
6. **Crossway Articles** — crossway.org/articles (ECU). Publisher's strong free article archive.
7. **Credo Magazine** — credomag.com (REF/BAP). Theology with classical/retrieval emphasis.
8. **Reformation21** — reformation21.org (REF; Alliance of Confessing Evangelicals).
9. **Founders Ministries** — founders.org (Reformed BAP). Confessional Baptist theology.
10. **Banner of Truth** — banneroftruth.org (REF/Puritan). Reformed/Puritan articles and texts.

### Reference, study & Bible tools
11. **Blue Letter Bible** — blueletterbible.org (ECU). Original-language tools, lexicons,
    commentaries, cross-references — excellent for word studies.
12. **Bible Hub** — biblehub.com (ECU). Parallel translations, interlinear, lexicons,
    commentaries side by side.
13. **Bible Gateway** — biblegateway.com (ECU). Many translations; fast text lookup/comparison.
14. **Precept Austin** — preceptaustin.org (ECU). Massive aggregation of commentary and
    verse-by-verse study notes from many trusted teachers.
15. **Christian Classics Ethereal Library (CCEL)** — ccel.org (ECU). Free full texts of
    Calvin, Luther, Edwards, Owen, the church fathers, etc.
16. **Monergism** — monergism.com (REF). Large free library of Reformed books, articles,
    and primary sources.
17. **The Bible Project** — bibleproject.com (ECU). Biblical-theology videos and study notes;
    strong on the Bible's literary storyline.
18. **StudyLight / Bible study commentaries** — studylight.org (ECU). Public-domain and modern
    commentaries, lexicons, and study tools.
19. **Spurgeon resources** — spurgeon.org (BAP; The Spurgeon Center). Sermons, *Morning &
    Evening*, and the *Treasury of David*.
20. **Reformed Confessions / Creeds reference** — ccel.org/creeds & thewestminsterstandard.org
    (REF). Authoritative texts of the historic confessions and catechisms.

### Apologetics & worldview
21. **Stand to Reason** — str.org (ECU; Greg Koukl). Clear-thinking apologetics and ethics;
    great method ("Columbo" tactics).
22. **Reasonable Faith** — reasonablefaith.org (ECU; William Lane Craig). Philosophical
    apologetics, scholarly Q&A.
23. **CARM** — carm.org (REF; Matt Slick). Apologetics, cults/world-religions comparison,
    doctrine; useful but check tone/edge-case claims.
24. **Got Questions** — gotquestions.org (ECU). Huge searchable Q&A on virtually any Bible

25. **BibleRef** — bibleref.com (ECU; from the GotQuestions team). Verse-by-verse explanations.

### Journals, seminaries & deeper study
26. **Tabletalk Magazine** — tabletalkmagazine.com (REF; Ligonier). Daily Bible studies and
    theme issues.
27. **Themelios (journal)** — thegospelcoalition.org/themelios (ECU). Free peer-reviewed
    evangelical theology and biblical studies.
28. **Reformed Theological Seminary — open resources** — rts.edu / itunes & Wisdom (REF).
    Free seminary-level lectures and articles.
29. **Westminster Theological Seminary** — wts.edu / faculty resources (REF). Scholarly
    Reformed theology and biblical studies.
30. **Christianity Today** — christianitytoday.com (ECU). Mainstream evangelical news,
    analysis, and theology across traditions; useful for breadth and current debates.

## Reliability & usage notes (important for a Q&A system)

- **Weighting:** Treat seminary/journal sources (Themelios, RTS/WTS, Ligonier, TGC, 9Marks,
  Crossway) and the systematic-theology books as *high-authority*. Treat aggregators
  (GotQuestions, CARM, Precept Austin) as *good first-pass* — accurate on mainstream points,
  but cite a primary source for contested or precise claims.
- **Show the tradition, not just "the answer."** For genuinely disputed doctrines, surface the
  REF / LUT / WES / BAP / ANG positions with their best biblical arguments rather than asserting
  one as *the* Protestant view.
- **Always anchor in the text.** The shared Protestant commitment is *sola scriptura*: the
  strongest answer cites Scripture in context first, then confessions/authors as secondary
  support.
- **Watch known biases:** Answers-in-Genesis-style young-earth claims, end-times charts, and
  charismatic/cessationist disputes are *intramural* — flag them as such.
- **Verify links periodically.** All URLs above were stable and reputable at compile time
  (2026-06); ministry sites occasionally restructure their article paths.
